Part C — Scenario: Who's more at risk?

⚠️ RetailCo Goes Into Administration

Scenario · Insolvency · Priority of claims

RetailCo has £200m in bonds (secured on warehouse properties) and £80m in debentures (unsecured). It enters administration. Properties sold for £180m. Who gets paid first — and what do debenture holders receive?

Bondholders are paid first. Because their bonds are secured on the properties, they have first claim on sale proceeds. £180m goes to bondholders — still £20m short of the £200m owed, so bondholders themselves take a loss. Debenture holders, being unsecured, receive nothing from the property sale. This is why debentures carry higher risk and higher interest — compensation for lower priority in insolvency.

Choosing Finance — What Matters?

Picking the right type of finance isn't random. Click each factor to reveal why it matters, then apply your knowledge to a real scenario.

⏱ 10 min👥 Team✦ Click + Scenario
⚖️
There is no universal "best" type of finance. The right choice depends on who the business is, what it needs the money for, how long it needs it, and what the economic environment looks like.
Part A — Click each factor to reveal why it matters

Cost: Debt has an explicit cost — the interest rate. Equity has an implicit cost — shareholders expect a return. When interest rates are high (e.g. 8%), taking on more debt becomes expensive and risky.
Use of Funds: Finance should match the asset it funds. Buying a factory (long-term asset) → use long-term finance. Covering a short-term cash gap → use an overdraft. Mismatching creates cash flow problems.
Status & Size: A large listed company can issue shares or bonds publicly. A small startup cannot. An established firm has credit history and assets — a new business often has neither.
Financial Situation: A company with strong profits can comfortably service debt. A loss-making company cannot pay interest (a legal obligation) and cannot rely on retained earnings.
External Environment: High interest rates make debt costly. In a recession, banks become risk-averse. The timing of financing decisions is strongly influenced by the economic cycle.
Organisational Goals: A company wanting rapid growth may prefer equity (no repayment pressure). One wanting to retain ownership may prefer debt.
Existing Financial Structure: Gearing matters. If a company is already 70% debt-funded, taking on more debt is dangerous — lenders may refuse or charge very high rates.
Risk: Debt creates mandatory cash outflows (interest). If profits fall, obligations remain. Equity carries no mandatory payments — dividends can be withheld if needed.
Availability of Security: Secured loans require assets as collateral. A manufacturer with property can offer security — lowering lender risk and securing a better rate. A software company with no physical assets cannot.
Part B — Apply the factors

🏗 BuildRight Construction — Quick Decision Required

Scenario · Financing decision · Multiple factors at play

BuildRight needs £30m to purchase new machinery. Profitable, owns significant property, low gearing, interest rates just rose to 7%. Need the money within 3 months. Which TWO factors are most decisive — and what do they point to?

Security + Existing structure → take secured debt. BuildRight owns property (security available) and has low gearing (room for debt). They are profitable and can service the interest at 7%. A secured loan or mortgage is the most logical choice. Equity would take too long (an IPO or rights issue cannot be done in 3 months) and would dilute existing shareholders unnecessarily.

🏦 FinanceCo — Debt: £140m · Equity: £60m

Scenario · Classify the capital structure

Specialist lender. Total debt £140m against equity £60m. Profits consistent but volatile — dependent on the credit cycle. What is their gearing and what does it mean?

Highly geared — 70% gearing. Debt ÷ (Debt + Equity) = 140 ÷ 200 = 70%. Given volatile profits, this structure is risky — a downturn could make interest payments difficult.

🛒 RetailPlc — Debt: £40m · Equity: £160m

Scenario · Classify the capital structure

Well-established supermarket chain with steady cash flows and significant property assets. Debt of £40m against equity of £160m. How geared are they?

Low geared — 20% gearing. Debt ÷ (Debt + Equity) = 40 ÷ 200 = 20%. RetailPlc could comfortably take on significantly more debt if needed.

⚖️
Ask before recommending: 1. Can they pay interest? 2. Do they have security? 3. How much debt already? 4. What stage are they? 5. What are market conditions?

🏭 MegaBuild Ltd — £40m Factory

Profitable · Low gearing · Owns £60m property · Interest rate: 5% · Strong cash flows

Established UK manufacturer, consistent profits, significant property assets, very little existing debt. Want to build a new £40m factory. Bank willing to lend at 5%.

Best answer: Secured Debt. Strong cash flows cover the interest. Property provides excellent security. Low existing gearing means there's room for more debt. A share issue would dilute existing shareholders unnecessarily. Remember: interest on the £40m loan is a finance cost in the P&L.

🚀 NovaTech — AI Startup, Needs £5m

2 years old · No profit · No assets · Banks declined · High-growth potential

Promising AI product but zero profits and nothing to offer as security. Three banks have already said no. Need £5m to hire engineers and scale up.

Only option: Venture Capital (Equity). No assets = no security for a loan. No profits = can't make interest payments. No retained earnings. VCs accept high risk in exchange for shares, betting on future growth.

🌿 GreenEnergy Plc — Already Listed, Tough Conditions

Public company · Share price −30% · Gearing: 60% · Interest rates: 8% · Needs £20m

Listed on the stock exchange. Wants to build a wind farm but faces a 30% share price drop, 8% interest rates, and is already 60% debt-funded. Every option has a downside.

No perfect answer — this is intentional. More debt at 8% with 60% gearing risks financial distress. A rights issue is viable even with a falling price. Waiting avoids both risks but delays the project. Real CFOs face exactly this.
